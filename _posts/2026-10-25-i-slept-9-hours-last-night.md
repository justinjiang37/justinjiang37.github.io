I slept 9 hours last night.

I feel well rested. After a week of consistently working out, sticking to my diet, and reaching my daily macros, I’m very content with the effort I’ve put into my physical health. However, as a junior studying computer engineering and computer science, I also feel a lot of guilt.

What I see on X, Instagram, and LinkedIn makes me inadvertently conform to the idea that I, as a university student, should be getting 5 hours of sleep on average, gulping down 3 Red Bulls after midnight, and constantly orchestrating a team of 5 coding agents. Whether it be some YC founders claiming they ate cake for breakfast or a Berkeley student who’s on their 3rd all-nighter after a back-to-back hackathon, the content I’m semi-voluntarily being fed with is directly clashing with what I have been doing for the past week. It feels like I’m enveloped by highway billboards shoving the idea of hustle culture being undoubtedly the optimal way to live life in my face.

While I’ve definitely been in that place before—during finals or grinding out labs and projects—it just doesn’t feel necessary at this time. I’m very comfortable with the way I’m living. I’m experiencing less brain fog, I feel great after a morning run, and I can get my work done on time. I’m relatively less distracted and, as I mentioned before, feel great physically.

I believe that hustle culture is only necessary when a clear direction is fueled by enough ambition, not when your friends are doomscrolling in the basement of a library at 3AM working on an assignment that can be easily done in an hour.

What’s exciting is that I’ve recently found a direction and it’s starting to come into focus. For the first time in a while, I’ve felt my childlike curiosity surface. (Here’s [what I’ve been working on](https://x.com/Jjiang37/status/2113508758899068984).)

Maybe this newfound interest will necessitate sleeping 5 hours. If so, so be it. Because despite what I’ve said in this blog post, I’m actually very jealous of those who are going to such lengths. I imagine that behind the exhaustion and cup noodle meals, they’re going to sleep thinking about their work, lighting up with excitement every time a wild hypothesis of theirs proves to be true, and smiling while problem solving because of a deep sense of fulfillment. I think I’m starting to feel that too.
